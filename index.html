    The Dragon Gate · 登竜門

    Four ages of a koi

    In Chinese legend, a carp that swims up the Yellow River and leaps the waterfall at Longmen, the Dragon Gate, becomes a dragon. Japanese turned the gate into a word, tōryūmon: the hard step that opens a career. Koi keepers name their fish by age, so every roadmap on this site uses the same four stages.

      Timelines assume about 8 to 10 hours a week next to school. Going faster or slower is normal.

      About & privacy

      Last updated October 15, 2026

      General guidance

      Ryūmon gives general guidance for exploring computing careers. It is not professional career, academic or financial advice. Course content, prices, admission rules and job markets change, so check details with the provider, your school or a counselor before you commit time or money.

      Independent

      Ryūmon is independent. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by any course provider, school, certification body or company it links to. Their names and trademarks belong to their owners. Linked sites have their own terms and privacy policies.

      Your privacy

      There are no accounts, forms, ads, cookies or analytics, and Ryūmon collects no personal information. Your saved paths, ticked steps and quiz answers are stored only in this browser and are never sent anywhere. Fonts are built into the page, so no font service sees your visit. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which logs visitors’ IP addresses for security when it serves the page, as most web hosts do. See the GitHub privacy statement.
